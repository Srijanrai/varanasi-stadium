The evening Ganga aarti at **Dashashwamedh Ghat starts at about 6 pm in winter and about 7 pm in summer**. It lasts around 45 minutes, and watching from the ghat steps is free. A smaller, calmer aarti takes place at Assi Ghat.

Below: the timings by season, whether you need a ticket, which ghat to pick, boat prices and how to get there.


## Ganga aarti at a glance

| | Dashashwamedh Ghat | Assi Ghat |
|---|---|---|
| Evening aarti | About 6 pm in winter, 7 pm in summer | After sunset |
| Morning | Aarti at about 5:30 am in winter, 5 am in summer | Subah-e-Banaras at sunrise |
| How long | About 45 minutes | Shorter |
| Crowd | Very crowded, more so on festivals | Lighter; easier with children or elderly parents |
| Cost to watch | Free | Free |
| Boats | Yes, moored off the ghat | Yes, rides start here |
| Nearby | Kashi Vishwanath, Godowlia | BHU, Lanka |

## Ganga aarti timing in winter and summer

The aarti is held at dusk, so it follows the season. The organisers, Ganga Seva Nidhi and Gangotri Seva Samiti, move it an hour earlier when the cold sets in.

| Aarti at Dashashwamedh | Winter | Summer |
|---|---|---|
| Evening | About 6:00 pm | About 7:00 pm |
| Morning | About 5:30 am | About 5:00 am |

There's no fixed changeover date. In 2025 the winter timing started on 7 November, when many Varanasi temples also shifted their aarti times by about 30 minutes.

## Do you need a ticket or booking?

No. The aarti is free, and you don't need to book anything to watch from the steps. There's no government ticket counter for it.

What you may be offered instead:

- **Boat seats** for watching from the river. These are private and paid.
- **Reserved seating or tour packages** sold by agencies. These are private and optional.
- **"Official aarti tickets" or "special passes"** from people at the ghat. These are touts. Walk away.

## Dashashwamedh or Assi: which aarti should you watch?

**Choose Dashashwamedh** for your first visit and the full spectacle. Rows of priests swing tall brass lamps in time with chants, bells and conch shells, with thousands watching.

**Choose Assi** if you're with children or elderly parents, or you'd rather not stand in a crush. It's smaller and calmer, and it's the closest main ghat to BHU and Lanka.

![Priests swinging incense lamps during the aarti at Assi Ghat, Varanasi](/images/guides/ganga-aarti/aarti-assi-ghat.webp)

With a morning to spare, Assi's **Subah-e-Banaras** at sunrise is worth the early alarm: Vedic chanting, yoga and live classical music on the ghat.

## Watching from a boat: views and prices

From a boat moored just off the bank you see the whole line of priests at once, without fighting for space. From the steps you're closer and part of the crowd.

There's no official fixed boat rate. Prices commonly quoted:

| Boat | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Shared boat seat | About ₹150–500 per person |
| Private boat | About ₹1,200–6,000 per boat |

Prices rise on festivals and weekends. Agree the fare, and whether it covers the full aarti, before you get in.

![Pilgrims in boats on the Ganga waiting for the evening aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat](/images/guides/ganga-aarti/ganga-aarti-from-boats-dashashwamedh.webp)

## How to reach Dashashwamedh and Assi for the aarti

Cars can't enter the lanes near the ghats. Take a cab or auto to **Godowlia crossing**, then walk about 500 m to Dashashwamedh. That's usually under 10 minutes, but much slower in the evening crowd. For Assi, get dropped at Assi crossing and walk down to the ghat.

| From | To Dashashwamedh | To Assi |
|---|---|---|
| Varanasi Junction (Cantt) | About 6 km by road | About 6–8 km by road |
| The stadium at Ganjari | About 18 km by road | A little shorter |

**On a match day, don't try to fit the aarti in.** Ganjari is well outside the city, and both trips run through heavy evening traffic. Go the evening before the match or the evening after.

## Tips that save the evening

- **Arrive early.** At least 45 minutes before the start at Dashashwamedh for a place on the steps.
- **Festivals change everything.** On Dev Deepawali and other big festival evenings, the ghats fill up hours ahead. During the record crowds of January 2025, the Dashashwamedh aarti was even closed to the public for several days.
- **Keep valuables close** in the crowd, and agree any boat or rickshaw fare before you set off.
- **Dress modestly.** It's a religious ceremony, even if it feels like a show.
- **Seeing Kashi Vishwanath too?** It's right next to Dashashwamedh. Check the [temple timings and VIP darshan](/temples/kashi-vishwanath/) to plan both in one evening.
